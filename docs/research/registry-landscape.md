# Registry: landscape, positioning & MVP cut-line (#255)

Research for #255, done 2026-11-13. It answers: what is table stakes for gift registries and wish
lists, where do existing products fall short, how do they make money, and what should CardJoy's
Registry be and launch for.

**How much to trust this.** Desk research from web search, not hands-on use of each product. Many
sources are competitor blogs or comparison sites with an incentive, review aggregators, and
forum posts; claims are marked **[weak]** where only such sources said it. Fee and discount
figures change often; re-check a vendor's own terms before quoting a number. Several named products
(The Knot, Target and Walmart registries) were not researched individually.

## Recommendation in one page

1. **Launch for the birthday and holiday wish list, not the wedding or baby registry.** It is
   lightweight, recurs yearly, and sits next to what CardJoy already does best (birthday cards and
   reminders). Wedding and baby registries are won on cash funds, group gifting, and retailer
   completion discounts, which we have chosen not to build.
2. **Position on three things the big registries can't say:** no fees and no commissions to
   anyone, the owner never learns who claimed what until after the event (and guests never see
   each other's contact details), and it connects to the cards people already send.
3. **The real edge is cards and occasions.** Turn the registry into the middle of a loop: a card
   or invitation links to it, a reminder points at it, and afterwards the thank-you list sends
   thank-you cards. The last step is missing and is the strongest bundle available.
4. **MVP cut-line: what exists is enough to launch;** the next work is closing the loop, not adding
   surface area (section 5).
5. **The invitation wish list (#95) stays as it is.** An invitation can also link a standalone
   registry (#293). No migration, no merge: the two share one model already.

## 2. Where they fall short (and what is solid evidence)

All **[weak]**: forum posts and app-review summaries, not studies.

- **Duplicate gifts from stale status.** The recurring complaint: items bought but still shown as
  available, especially from third-party sellers, with delays of days. One AI-summarized review
  claimed about a fifth of Babylist users report items not marked purchased; treat as anecdote.
- **Out-of-stock and cancelled items** that the list still shows; registrants having to replace
  items constantly when they listed multiples.
- **Guests buying off-list.** Guests add their own items at checkout, so the couple's real wishes go
  unbought.
- **Returns are awkward:** a duplicate comes back as a gift card, and the giver is not told.
- **Spoilers and privacy:** owners seeing purchase details they didn't want; Amazon's address
  sharing drew criticism **[weak]**.
- **Fees and lock-in:** cash fund fees that surprise couples; discounts that apply only to the
  registry's own store.

What CardJoy already does about these: claims are a promise with a reminder and release (so stale
claims clear), prices carry a "may have changed" note and refresh in the background, surprise mode
and a private ship-to address are built in, any store works, and there are no fees.

What it does not do: tell owners an item is unavailable, let guests report a dead link, track
returns or duplicates after the fact, or help the owner thank people.

## 3. Positioning

**Statement.** *The wish list that works with the cards you already send. Free, with no commissions
and no accounts for the people buying. Nobody spoils the surprise, and your address stays yours.*

**Launch occasion: birthday and holiday.** Reasons: it is recurring (a yearly reset already ships),
lightweight (no cash funds needed), and CardJoy's strongest card occasion is birthdays, with
reminders already wired to registries. Baby and wedding registries are a harder fight against
products with retail stores, completion discounts, and cash funds, so treat them as a later,
opt-in expansion, not the launch story.

**Natural bundles (what exists vs. missing):**

| Bundle | Status |
|---|---|
| Invitation links a registry | Done (#293) |
| Group card or 1-on-1 card links a registry | Done (#268) |
| Birthday reminder points at the contact's registry | Done (#282) |
| **Thank-you list sends thank-you cards** | **Missing.** The best loop-closer: the data (who gave what, with email) and the product (a 1-on-1 card with a Thank You occasion) both exist |
| **A card's recipient can start their own list** | **Missing.** A growth loop: people who receive a birthday card are exactly the people with birthdays |
| Group card lets contributors see gift ideas | Missing (lower value: contributors are guests of the card, not buyers of the gift) |

## 4. Audience and kids' lists

Primary users: adults for themselves, and parents for children (a first birthday, a baby).
Children never have accounts: the owner is the adult, and guests are adults.

Guidance for kids' lists (not legal advice; check with counsel before targeting under-13s):

- Keep the child's data minimal: the list shows a title the parent writes, and the "owner first name"
  comes from the parent's account, not the child's.
- Don't collect anything from a child directly. Guest emails are adults' and are now erased a week
  after the event.
- Don't market to under-13s; the product is for parents and adults.

## 5. MVP cut-line and next work

**Enough to launch** (all shipped): any-URL add with a bookmarklet, share target and bulk paste;
claim with reminder and release; surprise mode; private ship-to address; passcode; cover image;
QR code; sections; event reminders; archive after the event and email erasure; links from cards,
invitations and reminders; templates by occasion; owner insights.

**Explicitly out:** cash funds, pooled gifts, co-owners, affiliate links, retailer imports (see the
decisions on #253/#254 and the #257 research).

**Next, in order, each small enough to ship alone:**

1. **Thank-you tracking and "send a thank-you card".** Check off who has been thanked; one click
   opens a prefilled 1-on-1 thank-you card for that guest. Closes the loop and is the clearest use
   of "cards + occasions".
2. **"I already bought this" at claim time.** A checkbox that claims and marks bought in one step,
   which also helps the stale-status problem (fewer claims left hanging).
3. **Owner can pause an item** ("no longer available"), so guests aren't sent to dead or sold-out
   items and the owner doesn't have to delete the item (and its claims) to hide it.
4. **Let a card's recipient start their own wish list.** A call to action on a received card,
   pre-selecting the occasion.

**Not now (revisit with evidence):** guest "report a broken link", return/duplicate tracking, group
card gift ideas.

## Open questions this research did not close

- Hands-on use of each product, including whether guests need accounts (several answers above are
  "not confirmed").
- The Knot, Target and Walmart registries were not looked at individually.
- Real usage data for CardJoy: which occasions owners pick, claim rates, how many cards link a
  registry. These should decide the launch focus more than any competitor survey; the
  recommendation above is a prior, not a result.
